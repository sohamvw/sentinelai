# Information Technology Act, 2000 (as amended by IT (Amendment) Act, 2008)
Source: The Gazette of India, Ministry of Law and Justice.
Authority: Parliament of India. Applies to: All "Indian General" software,
e-commerce, and SaaS products serving Indian users. SentinelAI enforces these
rules when sector includes "indian_general".

## Section 43 — Penalty for damage to computer, computer system, etc.
Any person who accesses or downloads data without authorization, or damages
the system, is liable to pay damages.

## Section 43A — Compensation for failure to protect data (CRITICAL)
A body corporate that possesses, deals, or handles any sensitive personal
data or information, and is negligent in implementing reasonable security
practices, causing wrongful loss or wrongful gain, is liable to pay damages.

## Section 66 — Computer related offences
If a person dishonestly or fraudulently commits any act referred to in
Section 43, they are liable to imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to
₹5 lakh, or both.

## Section 66B — Punishment for dishonestly receiving stolen computer resource
Imprisonment up to 3 years, or fine up to ₹1 lakh, or both.

## Section 66C — Identity theft
Fraudulently or dishonestly making use of the electronic signature, password,
or any other unique identification feature of any other person. Imprisonment
up to 3 years and fine up to ₹1 lakh.

## Section 66D — Cheating by personation using communication device/computer
Imprisonment up to 3 years and fine up to ₹1 lakh.

## Section 66E — Violation of privacy
Capturing, publishing, or transmitting the image of a private area of any
person without consent. Imprisonment up to 3 years or fine up to ₹2 lakh.

## Section 66F — Cyber terrorism
Imprisonment that may extend to imprisonment for life.

## Section 67 — Publishing information which is obscene in electronic form
Imprisonment up to 5 years and fine up to ₹10 lakh on first conviction.

## Section 69 — Interception, monitoring, decryption
Government can intercept, monitor, or decrypt any information generated,
transmitted, stored, or hosted in any computer resource.

## Section 69B — Power to authorize to monitor and collect traffic data
For cyber security, the Government can authorize collection of metadata.

## Section 70 — Protected systems
The Central Government may declare any computer resource as a "protected
system" and prescribe rules for its access and security.

## Section 72 — Penalty for breach of confidentiality and privacy
Any person who secures access to any electronic record, book, register,
correspondence, information, document, or other material without the consent
of the person concerned, and discloses it to another person, is liable to
imprisonment up to 2 years, or fine up to ₹1 lakh, or both.

## Section 72A — Punishment for disclosure of information in breach of
lawful contract
Imprisonment up to 3 years, or fine up to ₹5 lakh, or both.

## SPDI Rules (2011) — Reasonable Security Practices
- A body corporate must have a privacy policy published on its website.
- Sensitive Personal Data or Information (SPDI) must be handled only with
  written consent, for a lawful purpose connected with a function or
  activity of the body corporate.
- SPDI must not be retained longer than necessary.
- A grievance officer must be appointed and contact details published.
- Body corporate must allow Data Principal to review and correct SPDI.
- Use of SPDI for targeted advertising requires prior consent.

## Penalties (Section 43A read with SPDI Rules)
Negligent handling of SPDI: damages up to ₹5 crore per instance.
